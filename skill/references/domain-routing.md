## Scripture and philosophy

Read:

- `source-policy.md`
- `daorenjia-source-guide.md` when a text may be in the Daoist canon

Determine whether the user wants:

- original text and textual variants;
- plain-language translation;
- comparison of commentaries;
- religious interpretation;
- historical/academic context;
- creative adaptation.

Do not silently switch among these frames.

## History, institutions, and lineage

Read:

- `source-policy.md`
- `daorenjia-source-guide.md` for canon titles, biographies, and historical collections
- `ritual-safety.md` when discussing ordination, registers, transmission, or practice

Separate:

- historical affiliation;
- influence or textual borrowing;
- modern religious identity;
- legally or institutionally recognized clerical status.

Do not infer lineage from a surname, family shrine, dream, talisman, heirloom, or self-description.

## Ritual and technique

Read:

- `source-policy.md`
- `daorenjia-source-guide.md`
- `ritual-safety.md`

Identify the likely textual family, time period, tradition, ritual function, and public status. Explain symbols and structure from public sources. Do not turn a historical text into modern step-by-step instructions without checking safety, qualifications, and context.

## Metaphysical systems

Read:

- `metaphysics.md`
- `ritual-safety.md`
- `source-policy.md` when textual claims are involved

Name the selected system before analysis. Ask for boundary conventions when a different rule changes the chart or conclusion.

## Creative adaptation

Read the domain references matching the source material.

Return three layers when authenticity matters:

1. `文献依据`: material directly grounded in a source;
2. `合成还原`: a plausible reconstruction from multiple traditions;
3. `艺术虚构`: newly created for story or screen.

Avoid presenting invented chants, seals, registers, or ritual procedures as authentic scripture.
