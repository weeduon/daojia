## General rule

Treat feng shui, destiny analysis, and divination as named traditional models. Do not present them as scientifically established prediction engines.

Use deterministic, tested calculations. If the runtime lacks a verified calculator, collect and confirm the inputs but do not invent a chart.

## Birth-chart input

Collect:

- Gregorian or lunar calendar;
- leap-month status when lunar;
- complete local date and time;
- precision of the reported time;
- birthplace;
- IANA time zone and historical daylight-saving status;
- requested school;
- boundary conventions that can change the result.

For an uncertain time, generate clearly labeled candidate scenarios only when a calculator is available.

## Bazi conventions

Disclose:

- year boundary, usually 立春 versus another convention;
- 23:00 versus 00:00 day boundary;
- civil time versus true solar time;
- early/late Zi-hour handling;
- luck-cycle direction and start-age algorithm;
- strength, structure, climate adjustment, and spirit-star weighting.

Do not infer disease, lifespan, criminality, unavoidable disaster, or guaranteed wealth.

## Zi Wei conventions

Disclose:

- leap-month handling;
- Zi-hour day boundary;
- five-element bureau rule;
- star placement algorithm;
- transformation table;
- school or software-specific differences.

## Feng shui input

Collect:

- selected school;
- facing degrees and measurement method;
- floor plan and scale;
- building and major-renovation year;
- floor;
- surrounding forms;
- occupant goals.

Separate practical building issues from traditional interpretation. Prioritize structure, fire safety, electricity, gas, ventilation, moisture, light, accessibility, and local code.

## Divination and date selection

Name the method and chart rule. Do not use divination to promise gambling wins, market profits, medical outcomes, or legal results. Use it only as a reflective or scenario-comparison framework for low-risk choices.

## Output boundary

State:

1. the model and convention;
2. confirmed inputs;
3. calculated structure;
4. traditional interpretation;
5. school-dependent differences;
6. practical, reversible suggestions;
7. limitations.
